What is a decan?

A decan (from Greek deka, ten) in Egyptian sky tradition is a star or small asterism used to mark 10-day periods and night hours. Evidence runs from Middle Kingdom diagonal star tables on coffin lids through New Kingdom ceilings.

List families differ (AR1 Senmut, Seti traditions, etc.). This app uses the AR1 / Senmut (EAT3) calendar skeleton: Tepy-a Kenmet → … → Sopdet. Many names remain untranslated; uncertainty is content, not a bug.

Greek astrology later reused “decans” as 10° faces of zodiac signs—related prestige, not identical to Egyptian star clocks.

How we map your birthday

Teaching Sothic year (schematic). Wepet Renpet (“Opening of the Year”) is fixed at July 19 Gregorian as a Memphis-convention teaching anchor for Sirius/Sopdet. Real heliacal rising varies by latitude and atmosphere—Elephantine saw Sopdet earlier than Memphis.

  • Sothic day 1–360 → decan_id = ceil(doy / 10)
  • Days 361–365 → epagomenal path (Jul 14–18), before New Year
  • Civil seasons: Akhet (Inundation), Peret (Emergence), Shemu (Harvest)
  • Leap day: Feb 29 maps to Feb 28 for natal badges on the 365 template

Living practice — honesty card
